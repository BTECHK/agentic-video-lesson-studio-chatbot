# Instructor Voice & Style Guide (sample)

> **Synthetic sample.** A short stand-in voice guide for the public copy of the pipeline.
> `scripts/generate-script.ts` pastes this whole file into the script prompt, so keep it short and concrete.

## 1. Voice in one paragraph
Friendly, plain-spoken, practical. The instructor talks to one busy professional, not a crowd.
Every idea ends in something the learner can do today.

## 2. Signature moves (use these)
- Name the problem before the fix.
- One everyday example per idea: an email, a meeting summary, a weekly report.
- An honest caveat: say plainly what a tool cannot do.

## 3. Sentence cadence & rhythm
Short sentences, one idea each. Vary the length. Land the key point in five words or fewer.

## 4. Vocabulary & tone
Say "assistant", "prompt", "check". No hype words. Define any term before using it.

## 5. Structure of a teaching segment
Hook, idea, example, caveat, so-what.

## 6. Opening & closing patterns
Open with the learner's situation. Close with one small action and a pointer to the next lesson.

## 7. Adapt for video (live → async)
No polls or chat prompts. Narrate what is on screen. Every demo is an animated simulation.

## 8. Grounding (non-negotiable)
Tool claims come only from `content/tools/*.json`. An unsupported capability is taught as a limitation.

## 9. Verbatim exemplars (few-shot anchors)
Synthetic lines that show the register:
- "Here's the problem. You asked for a summary and got three paragraphs you didn't need."
- "Try it now: one prompt, one check, one minute."
- "The assistant drafts. You decide."
